Operating Principle
Alerted on assignment, not by scanning the inbox. The future state reduces shared-inbox ambiguity by routing emails, forms, estimates, proofs, and invoices to a visible Company/Contact/Job structure.
Inquiry→Route / Assign→Gather Info→Quote→Approve / Design→Produce→Invoice / Close
Current State Pain Points
  • Shared order@ inbox requires multiple staff members to scan and self-assign work.
  • Ownership can become unclear when everyone sees every reply.
  • InfoFlow and Outlook do not anchor around a shared job record.
  • Walk-in and phone inquiries often begin as handwritten notes.
  • Missing details are chased manually by email.
  • QuickBooks deposit reconciliation remains a Phase 2 pain point.
Future State Outcomes
  • One Company and Job record anchors each request.
  • Routing replaces shared-inbox scramble.
  • Intake forms replace handwritten notes where useful.
  • Quote and proof approvals become separate gates.
  • Rush, reorder, and priority keywords become visible.
  • Dashboards support operations, production, finance, and leadership.
Discovery Findings
Client information retained and expanded in the original visual format.
Current Systems
  • Outlook / order@ for intake
  • InfoFlow / IFP for estimating, order confirmation, and invoicing
  • QuickBooks for accounting and deposit reconciliation
  • Fiery + barcode/cutter production automation
  • Client folders for historical proofs and reorder references
People / Roles Identified
  • Katie — primary intake
  • Zach — workflow/operations lead
  • Ben — estimating/pricing and first escalation
  • Jason — wide format / EDDM / production-specific work
  • Lee — owner, leadership approval, final escalation
Constraints
  • Preserve InfoFlow in Phase 1.
  • Do not disrupt existing production automation.
  • Keep walk-in intake fast.
  • Do not rely on memory for follow-ups.
  • Keep quote approval and proof approval separate.
